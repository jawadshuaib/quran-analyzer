## 1. Identity, title, author, dates

1. **Title and full title.** "An Arabic-English Lexicon, derived from the best and the most copious Eastern sources; comprising a very large collection of words and significations omitted in the Kamoos, with supplements to its abridged and defective explanations, ample grammatical and critical comments, and examples in prose and verse … by Edward William Lane. In two books: the first containing all the classical words and significations commonly known to the learned among the Arabs: the second, those that are of rare occurrence and not commonly known. Book I.—Part 1 … Williams and Norgate … 1863." — title page, lane-preface scan (VOL. 1 OCR lines ~70–120).
2. **Arabic title مدّ القاموس.** Preface p. xxxii: "The Arabic title مَدُّ القَامُوس (which Arabs in general, in the present day … would pronounce 'Medd el-Ḳámoos' …) I have adopted in imitation of that given to his lexicon by El-Feyroozábádee. It has two meanings: 'The Flow of the Sea' and 'The Extension of the Ḳámoos.'" (Arabic as in the github transcription; OCR of the print garbles the Arabic but has the transliteration and glosses.) → used for `titleAr` / `titleGloss`.
   - **Site data issue:** the `dictionaries.name_ar` field holds the English "Lane's Lexicon", not an Arabic title. Lane's own Arabic title is مدّ القاموس.
3. **Author.** Edward William Lane, b. 17 Sept. 1801 at Hereford, d. 10 Aug. 1876 (DNB). Lane-Poole's Editor's Preface (image): "Mr. Lane died, on the tenth of August, 1876." Site label "Edward William Lane" is correct; the slug reverses the names (cosmetic). Stored date 1876 = death year, correct.
4. **Parts and dates.** DNB: first part 1863; "The succeeding parts came out in 1865, 1867, 1872, 1874, and posthumously, under the editorship of S. Lane-Poole (unfortunately with unavoidable lacunæ), in 1877, 1885, and 1892." **Disagreement:** Part 8's title page reads 1893 and Lane-Poole's Postscript in it is dated "1st January, 1893"; Arberry (p. 116) also gives 1893. Guide uses 1893. Part 5 title page: 1874 (VOL. 5 OCR). Part 6 = غ and ف (Editor's Preface image: "The present Part therefore contains only غ and ف"). Part 7 announced as ق ك ل م and Part 8 as ن ه و ي (Editor's Preface image). Part 8 title page: "BOOK I.—PART 8 … AND SUPPLEMENT. EDITED BY STANLEY LANE-POOLE … 1893" (VOL. 8 OCR).
5. **Funding / origin.** Preface p. v: "IN the year 1842, a most generous offer made to me by the present Duke of Northumberland (then Lord Prudhoe) enabled me to undertake the composition of this work."
6. **Seven years in Cairo.** p. xxii: "For seven years, in Cairo, I prosecuted my task …"; p. xxiii: "After a stay of somewhat more than seven years in Cairo". DNB: October 1842 – October 1849. **Thirty-four years of labour:** Lane-Poole, Editor's Preface: "After thirty-four years of labour at the Lexicon, Mr. Lane died" (image).

## 2. Purpose, sources, audience (Lane's own words)

7. **Aim vs Golius.** p. v: "The object proposed was not to do in English little more than what Golius and others had already done in Latin, by translating and composing from a few Arabic lexicons of the class of epitomes or abstracts or manuals; but to draw chiefly from the most copious Eastern sources".
8. **Tāj al-ʿArūs described.** p. vi: "This work, entitled 'Taj el-'Aroos' … a compilation from the best and most copious Arabic lexicons, in the form of a running commentary on the Kamoos".
9. **Tāj as medium.** p. xix: "As the Taj el-'Aroos is the medium through which I have drawn most of the contents of my lexicon, I must more fully state the grounds upon which I determined to make so great a use of it."
10. **Lisān discovery.** p. xx: "in comparing large portions of it with the corresponding portions of the Lisán el-'Arab, I made the unexpected discovery that, in most of the articles in the former, from three-fourths to about nine-tenths of the additions to the text of the Kámoos, and in many articles the whole of those additions, existed verbatim in the Lisán el-'Arab … This circumstance has induced me very often to compose articles of my lexicon principally from the Lisán el-'Arab in preference to the Taj el-'Aroos, comparing the contents afterwards with the latter; and when they agreed, giving the latter as my authority in most instances (though not always)". → Important for readers: a "TA" tag may cover material Lane took from the Lisān.
11. **Transcription and al-Dasūqī.** p. xxi: copy "in twenty-four thick quarto-volumes"; "The transcription of my own copy, and its collation, extended over a period of more than thirteen years … the sheykh Ibráheem Ed-Dasookee … Upon him the task of transcription mainly devolved; and the collation was performed wholly by him in conjunction with myself or with another sheykh." p. v introduces him ("the sheykh Ibráheem (surnamed 'Abd-el-Ghaffár) Ed-Dasookee").
12. **Other lexicons at hand.** p. xxii: "While mainly composing from the Taj el-'Aroos, I have often had before me, or by my side, eight or ten other lexicons". p. v: two copies of the Ṣiḥāḥ and a Qāmūs from Fresnel. p. xv (Mughrib, bracketed Lane note): "The 'Mughrib' of El-Muṭarrizee, who was born in Khuwárezm, in the year of the Flight 536, and died in 610. This is a lexicon of select words and phrases, and particularly of such as occur in books of Traditions, and other works relating to the law … I have constantly consulted it and drawn from it". p. xvi (Miṣbāḥ): "'El-Miṣbáḥ el-Muneer fee Ghareeb esh-Sharḥ el-Kebeer.' This is a lexicon similar to the Mughrib … much more comprehensive … Its author states in it that he finished its composition in the year of the Flight 734." (734 AH = 1333–4 CE — my conversion.)
13. **English, not Latin.** p. xxi: "I did not hesitate to write my lexicon in English rather than in Latin, because the latter language is not sufficiently perspicuous nor sufficiently copious."
14. **Two Books.** pp. xxi–xxii: about a third of his material was rare words; "I therefore finally determined to divide my lexicon into two Books: the first to contain all the classical words and significations commonly known to the learned among the Arabs: the other, those that are of rare occurrence and not commonly known."
15. **Audience.** p. xxvii: "I have supposed the student who will make use of this work to be acquainted with the general rules of grammar." Lane-Poole's Postscript calls it "a Lexicon intended mainly for the use of scholars". (Also p. xi, n. 7, on the value of such lexicons "in interpreting the Hebrew Scriptures" — noted, not used in the guide.)

## 3. Method and conventions (Lane's own words)

16. **Explanations in the transmitters' words.** p. xxii: "the strict rules of Arabic lexicology demand that every explanation be given as nearly as possible in the words in which some person of authority has transmitted it".
17. **Square brackets.** p. xxvi: "I have inserted nothing in my lexicon without indicating at least one authority for it, except interwoven additions of my own which I have invariably distinguished by enclosing them between square brackets."
18. **Authorities agree "essentially".** p. xxvi: "When two or more indications of authorities are given, it is to be understood that they agree essentially, or mainly; but not always that they agree in words. When any authority is, in an important degree, less full, or less clear, than another … I distinguish it by an asterisk placed after the initial" (runs onto p. xxvii). Also p. xxvi: after Part 1 he gave fewer authorities; "what is most important is to note the oldest authority mentioned in any of my originals, with one or more of good repute to confirm it."
19. **Modern Arabic knowledge + caution.** pp. xxii–xxiii: "many [words rendered] by words meant to be understood in post-classical senses. In these last cases, I have often found in my knowledge of modern Arabic a solution of a difficulty: but without great caution, such knowledge would frequently have misled me, in consequence of the changes which have taken place in the applications of many words since the classical age."
20. **Against analogy.** p. xxiii: "Great caution is likewise requisite in the attempt to elicit the significations of words by means of analogy; as I could easily show by giving all the principal words of one article with their significations, and then requiring any student to divine the significations of the other words of the same article by such means, and comparing his explanations with those that have been authoritatively transmitted."
21. **Tropical marks, genealogies, dashes.** p. xxv: "Whenever I have found it possible to do so, I have distinguished (by the mark ‡) what is affirmed to be tropical from what is proper; generally on the authority of the Asás. I have also generally distinguished (by the mark †) what I regard as evidently, or probably, tropical, when I have found no express authority for asserting such to be the case. Thus I have often been enabled to draw clearly what may be termed the 'genealogies' of significations. Always, in the arrangement of significations, I have, to the utmost of my ability, paid attention to their relations, one to another. The mark — is used to denote a break in the relations of significations &c.; and ═ denotes an extraordinary, or a complete, dissociation." (Which dagger is which: the github transcription and Table II (p. xxix) agree: "‡ means asserted to be tropical … † means supposed by me to [be] tropical"; OCR of p. xxv shows two different glyphs in the same order.)
22. **"or".** p. xxv: "When several significations are assigned to one word &c., connected by 'or,' it is often the case that one is right in one instance, and another in another; and not unfrequently, that all are correct in different instances."
23. **Arrow.** p. xxiv: "(In order to facilitate the reference, an arrow-head (↓) is inserted to render conspicuous a word explained in a paragraph headed by another word.)"
24. **Abbreviations of terms** (Table II, pp. xxviii–xxix): "Aor., for aorist, مُضَارِعٌ"; "Inf. n., for infinitive noun, مَصْدَرٌ"; "I. q., for idem quod"; "Q. v., for quod vide"; "Syn., for synonym"; "Post-classical, مُوَلَّدٌ and مُحْدَثٌ"; "Tropical, مَجَازٌ"; "‡ means asserted to be tropical. ‡‡ … doubly tropical. † means supposed by me to [be] tropical."
25. **Authorities** (Table IV, p. xxxi): A = the "Asás" of Ez-Zamakhsheree; Bḍ = El-Beyḍáwee's "Exposition of the Ḳur-án"; Jel = the "Exposition of the Ḳur-án" by the Jeláleyn; Ḳ = the "Ḳámoos"; Ḳur = the "Ḳur-án"; L = the "Lisán el-'Arab"; M = the "Moḥkam"; Mgh = the "Mughrib" of El-Muṭarrizee; Mṣb = the "Miṣbáḥ" of El-Feiyoomee; Ṣ = the "Ṣiḥáḥ"; T = the "Tahdheeb" of El-Azheree; TA = the "Táj el-'Aroos"; O = the "'Obáb" of Eṣ-Ṣaghánee; KT = the Kitáb et-Taạreefát; Kh = El-Khaleel; I'Ab = Ibn-'Abbás; AO = Aboo-'Obeydeh; Akh = El-Akhfash; IbrD = Ibráheem Ed-Dasooḳee; CK = the Calcutta edition of the Ḳámoos; TK = the Turkish Translation of the Ḳámoos. p. xxx: Table III, a chronological list of lexicologists and grammarians (e.g. "El-Beyḍáwee 685 or 690 or 691").
26. **Classical vs post-classical.** p. viii: the Arabs sought "to draw a distinct line between the classical and post-classical languages. For the former language was that of the Ḳur-án and of the Traditions of Moḥammad …". p. ix: only a "Jáhilee" or "Mukhaḍram" poet is an absolute authority; an "Islámee" is not cited "absolutely and unquestionably"; a "Muwelled" is "absolutely post-classical"; "the classical age may be correctly defined as having nearly ended with the first century" [of the Flight]; "The highest of all authorities, however, on such points, prosody of course excepted, is held by the Arabs to be the Ḳur-án."
27. **Exclusions.** p. xxvi: "Proper names of persons and of places, and post-classical words and significations, I have, with very few exceptions, excluded from my lexicon."

## 4. The posthumous parts

28. **Editor's Preface (July 1877; page image).** "I found articles in three different stages: some consisting only of Mr. Lane's own notes, without any reference to the original authorities; others written, but needing to be collated with one or two manuscripts acquired later; and some completely written and ready for the press. The difference is explained by the fact that Mr. Lane was of necessity obliged to write in the order of the Ṣiḥáḥ, and that as the printers gradually approached him he finished those articles which were likely to be speedily wanted: for he began to print when he had written rather more than half the work." / "At the time of his death my Uncle was engaged on the article قد. Up to this point every article is ready for the printers. Of the rest the majority are written, but some need collation." / "…publish in Part VI. only to the end of the letter ف … The present Part therefore contains only غ and ف. Up to p. 2386 the proofs were corrected by Mr. Lane; after that, by myself. The next Part … will contain ق, ك, ل, م; and the last, ن, ه, و, ى."
   - This explains our data: in letters ق–ي, long full articles (e.g. كفر, قرب, قلب) sit beside short notes (e.g. قتل, كفى) — Lane wrote by final radical (the Ṣiḥāḥ order), not alphabetically.
29. **Postscript (1 January 1893; page image).** "The present Part completes the publication of the MS. materials which Mr. Lane left … My original intention was to compile the missing articles from Mr. Lane's MS. copy of the Táj-el-'Aroos … This intention, however, was modified when the publication of the Táj-el-'Aroos at Booláḳ deprived Mr. Lane's MS. copy of its peculiar importance … the Supplement appended to the present Part, instead of containing all the articles omitted from Parts V.—VIII., includes only such notes as Mr. Lane had made from time to time with a view to the eventual writing of these articles. These notes are not to be accepted as the final decision of their writer, far less as finished or even approximately complete articles. They have not enjoyed the benefit of his revision, and many of them are clearly the record of contemporary speech, which he would doubtless have excluded from a Lexicon of the classical language … The notes refer chiefly to the less common meanings, and those most familiar to the student will often be found missing." / "With regard to the 'Book II.' … I am obliged to admit that the materials are wanting." (Dozy's *Supplément* named as making it less needed.)
30. **Haywood (1960), pp. 124–25:** Lane decided to divide the work into two books; "At the time of his death, he was working on Book I. on the root 'qadda'. Book II was not properly started at all. Parts (volumes) I to VI—up to and including the letter fa'—were published between 1863 and 1877. In the Preface to Volume VI, his nephew, Stanley Lane-Poole, wrote: 'Of the rest (of the articles), the majority are written but need some collation'. When Volumes VII and VIII were published in 1885 and 1893, [they] showed how inaccurate this statement was. Lane-Poole had given up any serious attempt to supplement his uncle's notes, which were very incomplete for the rest of the lexicon: in fact he published them just as they were. [The] Lexicon is, therefore, a very inadequate reference work after the article 'qadda'." (OCR; Haywood misquotes Lane-Poole slightly — the original says "some need collation".) **Disagreement recorded:** Lane-Poole (1877) "majority are written" vs Haywood's verdict; our data shows both full articles and notes in ق–ي.
31. **Haywood pp. 125–26:** a committee (Cambridge 1954) decided that the arrangement of August Fischer's material in Tübingen under J. Kraemer "should concentrate on the letter kaf and what followed so as to supplement Lane's Lexicon. The first fascicule of the new dictionary, beginning kaf, was published in 1957." Also: Dozy's *Supplément* "aimed to supplement Lane" (p. 126). Guide uses only the kāf/1957 point, attributed to Haywood.
32. **Arberry (1960), pp. 115–16:** "At the time of his last illness Lane was engaged upon putting the finishing touches to his annotations on the root QD … he was thus spared to complete about three quarters of Book I of his Lexicon and for the remainder he left notes in varying stages of completion. He himself corrected the proofs up to page 2386. Lane-Poole brought out Part VI in 1877, Part VII in 1885 and Part VIII including a supplement, in 1893—a total of 3064 pages. The entries from QD onwards were left defective. The projected second book containing the rarer words never saw the light." (Arberry's praise of the work is not used.)
33. **Supplement p. 2984 (page image):** running head "قتل — قبل … [Supplement."; article قتل: "1. قَتَلَ الشَّىْءَ, inf. n. قَتْلٌ, †He knew the thing; he was, or became, acquainted with it: (Mṣb:) [or rather, i. q.] قَتَلَهُ عِلْمًا, (Bḍ in iv. 156, and TA,) …". OCR of the same volume shows "SUPPLEMENT TO PARTS VII. AND VIII." as the heading of this section. → Our stored qtl entry = this Supplement note, word for word.

## 7. Site-data / rendering issues found (for the return value, not the guide)

- `dictionaries.name_ar` = "Lane's Lexicon" (English); Lane's Arabic title is مدّ القاموس (preface p. xxxii).
- The English versions (faithful translation and harmonized) of many Lane entries reproduce Lane's verse numbers as if standard: 900 harmonized entries contain n:n references; e.g. Swm harmonized "Qur'an 2:181" and "Qur'an 19:27" (= Q 2:185, Q 19:26); qtl harmonized "Qurʾan 4:156" (= Q 4:157). If these are auto-linked, readers land on the wrong verse.
- qtl harmonized expands "( Bd , K , TA )" as "(Baydawi …, Kashshaf, Taj al-ʿArus)": K is the Qāmūs, not the Kashshāf (Ksh).
- Swm harmonized ends "Throughout, Lane traces every sense back to a single underlying idea: standing still, holding back, or abstaining." Not in Lane's text (the entry marks complete dissociations A2–A4, e.g. the tree called ṣawm); it also says "Lane reports this as the primary, original meaning" where the original credits TA and Msb.
- Rendering: `DictionaryGuideProse` renders any :::excerpt in a Lane/Salmoné guide as LTR English text (isArabic requires sourceLanguage 'ar'), so an Arabic excerpt from a comparison dictionary would display badly; the guide therefore quotes al-Rāghib inline instead of in an excerpt block.

## 8. Open questions / evidence gaps

- Which Qur'an verse-numbering Lane followed (likely Flügel 1834) — not confirmed from a source I could open.
- No modern (post-1980) scholarly study of Lane's lexicographical method was consulted (Baalbaki 2014 search gave nothing; Leila Ahmed 1978 and Jason Thompson 2010 not accessible). The account of method rests on Lane's own preface, Lane-Poole, Haywood and Arberry.
- The stored text cannot tell a reader which ق–ي entries are finished articles and which are Supplement notes; I verified only qtl. Lane-Poole's "majority are written" vs Haywood's "very inadequate" cannot be quantified from our data beyond the length statistics.
- Part 7's print (ق–م) was not opened; kfr's "CCC" and arrow placement not checked against it.
- DNB gives 1892 for Part 8; title page and Postscript give 1893 (guide uses 1893).

## 11. Added in revision round 1 (2026-09-26)

- **Ṣiḥāḥ arrangement.** Preface p. xiv (VOL. 1 OCR, between the running heads "xiv PREFACE." and "PREFACE. xv"): al-Jawharī composed it "mentioning each word according to the place of the last letter of the root, and then the first and second, in the usual order of the alphabet". This explains Lane-Poole's remark that Lane "was of necessity obliged to write in the order of the Sihah".
- **Printed p. 2487** (VOL. 3 file = Part 7, `_text.pdf` page 15, rendered and viewed). The top of the left column has the bracketed block "[قتع قتل قتم قتن قتو See Supplement.]", then the article قث. So قتل, which precedes قد in the printed order, was not written up. It exists only as a Supplement note (p. 2984).
  - Observation, not used in the guide: the roots skipped here all end in late letters (ع ل م ن و), while قتب, قتد, قتر on the facing page are written. This fits Lane-Poole's account that Lane wrote by final radical, but I have not checked it systematically.
- **Postscript, 1893** (VOL. 5 OCR ~l. 226–258): Supplement notes "have not enjoyed the benefit of his revision, and many of them are clearly the record of contemporary speech, which he would doubtless have excluded from a Lexicon of the classical language". Now used in the guide.
