## Claims and their support

### Author, dates, places
1. **Name**: Majd al-Dīn Abū l-Ṭāhir Muḥammad b. Yaʿqūb … al-Fīrūzābādī al-Shīrāzī. — al-Sakhāwī vol. 10 p. 79: «محمد بن يعقوب بن محمد بن إبراهيم بن عمر … المجد أبو الطاهر … الفيروزابادي الشيرازي اللغوي الشافعي». EI² abstract: "Abu ’l-Ṭāhir Muḥammad b. Yaʿḳūb … Mad̲j̲d al-Dīn".
2. **Spelling** Fīrūzābādī (long ī): EI² abstract "al-Fīrūzābādī"; Baalbaki "Fīrūzābādī". Lane p. xvi: "El-Feyroozábádee, [or, as some pronounce it, El-Feeroozábádee]" — so Fayrūzābādī / Fīrūzābādī both occur. The site's "Firūzābādī" (short i + long ū) matches neither → siteDataIssue.
3. **Birth**: Rabīʿ II (or Jumādā II) 729 at Kāzarūn, near Shiraz — al-Sakhāwī p. 79 «ولد في ربيع الآخر وقيل في جمادى الآخرة سنة تسع وعشرين وسبعمائة بكازرون من أعمال شيراز»; EI² abstract: "born at Kāzarūn, a town near Shīrāz … Rabīʿ II or Djumādā II 729/February or April 1329". **Disagreement**: the Qāmūs itself (art. كرز, Risāla p. 522): «وكارَزِين: د بفارس … وبه وُلِدْتُ» ("Kārazīn: a town in Fārs … and there I was born"); Haywood p. 83 follows this ("he himself tells us that he was born in Karzin") and gives birth 726/1326. Guide says only "born in 729/1329 in Fārs, near Shiraz" — true on either account of the place; birth year per al-Sakhāwī/EI².
4. **Education/travels**: Shiraz from age 8, Wāsiṭ, Baghdad (745), Damascus (755), Jerusalem c. ten years, Cairo, Mecca; Rūm and India — al-Sakhāwī pp. 79–80; EI² abstract (Shiraz, Wāsiṭ, Baghdad 745/1344, Damascus 750/1349 — note EI² gives 750 for Damascus, al-Sakhāwī 755; not used in guide).
5. **Zabīd**: entered Zabīd Ramaḍān 796; al-Ashraf Ismāʿīl received him; made chief qāḍī of all Yemen 1 Dhū l-Ḥijja 797; held it >20 years until death — al-Sakhāwī p. 81 «ثم دخل زبيد في رمضان سنة ست وتسعين … فتلقاه الملك الأشرف إسماعيل … أضاف إليه قضاء اليمن كله … في أول ذي الحجة سنة سبع وتسعين … إلى حين وفاته وهي مدة تزيد على عشرين سنة». Haywood p. 84 same outline.
6. **Death**: night of 20 Shawwāl 817 at Zabīd, nearly 90 — al-Sakhāwī p. 86 «مات … في ليلة عشرى شوال سنة سبع عشرة بزبيد وقد ناهز التسعين». Tabular conversion: 20 Shawwāl 817 ≈ 2 January 1415 (Julian) → **1415 CE**. Baalbaki pp. 50, 385, 392: "d. 817/1415". Haywood p. 83: 817/1414. Lane p. xvi: "died in 816". Site stores 1414 → minor siteDataIssue (817 AH is mostly 1414, but the recorded day falls in Jan 1415).
7. **Composition in Mecca**: al-Sakhāwī p. 83 quoting al-Taqī al-Kirmānī: «ثم جاور بمكة مدة عشر سنين أو أكثر وصنف بها تصانيف منها … والقاموس مطولا في مجلدات عديدة ثم أمره والدي باختصاره فاختصر في مجلد ضخم وفيه فوائد عظيمة … واعتراضات على الجوهري». Haywood p. 84: "he spent ten years in Mecca. Here he wrote … his dictionary". Guide: "a contemporary quoted by al-Sakhāwī says it was written in Mecca, first at length, then abridged".
8. **In circulation by 790/1388**: al-Sakhāwī pp. 85–86 quoting al-Maqrīzī: «آخر ما اجتمع به في مكة سنة تسعين وقرأت عليه بعض مصنفاته وناولني قاموسه وأجازني». Also Ibn Ḥajar received most of it at Zabīd in 800 (p. 86).

### The preface (Risāla ed. pp. 25–32) — read in full
9. p. 26: «وكنت برهة من الدهر ألتمس كتابا جامعا بسيطا … ولما أعياني الطلاب، شرعت في كتابي الموسوم بـ"اللامع المعلم العجاب، الجامع بين المحكم والعباب"» — he began (شرعت) a work combining *al-Muḥkam* and *al-ʿUbāb*.
10. p. 27: «غير أني خمنته في ستين سفرا، يعجز تحصيله الطلاب، وسئلت تقديم كتاب وجيز على ذلك النظام … وألفت هذا الكتاب محذوف الشواهد، مطروح الزوائد، معربا عن الفصح والشوارد … ولخصت كل ثلاثين سفرا في سفر، وضمنته خلاصة ما في "العباب" و"المحكم"، وأضفت إليه زيادات … وسميته "القاموس المحيط"؛ لأنه البحر الأعظم». → quotation used in guide: «وَأَلَّفْتُ هَذَا الْكِتَابَ مَحْذُوفَ الشَّوَاهِدِ، مَطْرُوحَ الزَّوَائِدِ» (my translation: "I composed this book with the illustrative quotations removed and the superfluities thrown out").
11. p. 27: «ولما رأيت إقبال الناس على "صحاح" الجوهري، وهو جدير بذلك، غير أنه فاته نصف اللغة أو أكثر، إما بإهمال المادة، أو بترك المعاني الغريبة النادة … فكتبت بالحمرة المادة المهملة لديه» — red ink for roots al-Jawharī left out.
12. p. 27: «ومن أحسن ما اختص به هذا الكتاب: تخليص الواو من الياء» — separation of wāw/yāʾ roots. Baalbaki p. 394 (snippet) confirms: "observed the separation between wāw and yāʾ (takhlīṣ al-wāw min al-yāʾ) – an issue which, he says, has confused earlier authors." Haywood p. 86: not strictly new (Ibn Fāris had separated them).
13. p. 28 conventions: feminine «وهي بهاء»; verb given by maṣdar alone or past without present → like كتب; present without qualification → like ضرب; «وكل كلمة عريتها عن الضبط فإنها بالفتح إلا ما اشتهر بخلافه»; «مكتفيا بكتابة: ع، د، ة، ج، م، عن قولي: موضع، وبلد، وقرية، والجمع، ومعروف». al-Sakhāwī p. 84 adds جج for جمع الجمع («وعن جمع الجمع بجج»). Haywood pp. 86–87 same list; Haywood p. 87 on model words ("ka samiʿa").
14. p. 28 on al-Jawharī: «ثم إني نبهت فيه على أشياء ركب فيها الجوهري رحمه الله خلاف الصواب، غير طاعن فيه، ولا قاصد بذلك تنديدا له … واختصصت كتاب الجوهري من بين الكتب اللغوية … لتداوله واشتهاره بخصوصه، واعتماد المدرسين على نقوله ونصوصه». Guide paraphrases: flags where al-Jawharī "went against what is right", "not to disparage him", chose his book because teachers relied on it.
15. p. 32: «وكتابي هذا … صريح ألفي مصنف» — "the pure essence of two thousand works". (Haywood p. 87 says "a thousand books" — contradicts the preface; not used.) Baalbaki p. 393: additions "hardly substantiate its author's claim that it contains the digest of two thousand works (ṣarīḥ alfay muṣannaf)".

### The Lāmiʿ unfinished
16. al-Sakhāwī p. 82: «اللامع المعلم العجاب … يقدر تمامه في مائة مجلد … رأيت بخطه أيضا أنه كمل منه مجاليد خمسة». (Disagreement: 100 volumes here vs 60 in the preface.) Lane pp. xvii–xviii: the preface's words mean he "commenced (not … completed)" it; al-Fāsī and al-Suyūṭī say never completed. Baalbaki p. 392: "It is not clear how much he accomplished of that lexicon". Guide: "never finished — al-Sakhāwī says he saw in the author's hand that five volumes were done".

### Sources, scale, additions (scholarship)
17. Baalbaki p. 393: "al-Muḥkam and al-ʿUbāb are its two main sources, but … Fīrūzābādī sporadically adds lexical items or meanings … the additions … form only a modest part … Most of these additions are names of Companions of the Prophet, scholars of Ḥadīth, poets, place names and plants, in addition to medical terms … proper nouns … three times more than in al-Lisān and twenty times more than in al-Ṣaḥāḥ" (estimate credited to Wadgīrī 1987). Lane p. xvii: "mainly from the Moḥkam and 'Obáb".
18. Haywood p. 87: "omitted all reference to them and their authors, as well as the usual illustrative examples, to save space." — overstated: displayed entries sometimes name authorities (بعض: Ibn Darastawayh, Abū Ḥātim, Sībawayh, al-Akhfash; عين: al-Riyāshī; ذهب: «عن "النهاية"»). Guide says "usually gone".
19. Lane p. xvii: Qāmūs "little more than … an enormous vocabulary … with very few critical observations, many of which are false, and scarcely any examples from the poets"; explanations abridged "so as to render them unintelligible to the most learned of the Arabs"; criticisms of al-Ṣiḥāḥ inserted "without acknowledgement", many "borrowed from the Annotations on the Ṣiḥāḥ by Ibn-Barree and El-Busṭee, or from the Supplement … by Eṣ-Ṣaghánee … generally when they are false". Lane says he writes "in defence of El-Jowharee". → reported as Lane's view.
20. Haywood p. 75: "Aḥmad Faris al-Shidyaq … counted specimen sections of the 'Sahah' and the 'Qamus', and was surprised to discover that, excluding proper names, the 'Sahah' contained more entries" (Haywood cites Darwīsh p. 93). Baalbaki p. 393: al-Zabīdī counts 20,000 *mādda* in the Qāmūs not in al-Ṣiḥāḥ. → competing assessments of the "half the language" claim.
21. Haywood p. 87: polemic "resolved itself into Jauhari versus Firuzabadi" (not used).
22. Arrangement: Baalbaki p. 394 (snippet): "he arranged his sections (abwāb) and chapters (fuṣūl), as well as the roots, on the model that had been perfected by Ǧawharī". Risāla ed. p. 1242: «[باب الهاء] فصل الهمزة • أبهته … • أله …» — root أله sits in bāb al-hāʾ, faṣl al-hamza.
23. Word *qāmūs* → "dictionary": Baalbaki p. 392 "So popular was this lexicon that the word qāmūs came to denote 'lexicon' in general"; p. 50 (snippet) "introduced to lexicography only in the early ninth/fifteenth century following the title that Fīrūzābādī … gave". Haywood p. 83 similar.
24. Tāj al-ʿArūs: Baalbaki p. 398: "In his ziyādāt, Zabīdī primarily restores the material neglected by Fīrūzābādī for the sake of conciseness. This material includes the names of authorities to whom data is ascribed, the šawāhid and their renderers, and the missing roots, lexical items and meanings." Baalbaki p. 400 (snippet): quotes al-Qāmūs "in full and continuously interrupts to explain or expand". Lane p. xviii: "an interwoven commentary on the Ḳámoos; exhibiting fully and clearly, from the original sources, innumerable explanations which are so abridged in the latter work as to be unintelligible". Haywood p. 89 same.
25. Lane p. xxii (on the lexicons he worked from, discussing the Qāmūs in the same passage): synonyms explaining each other ("Numerous cases of this kind occur in the Ḳámoos"); "More frequently, in lieu of an explanation, we find merely the word مَعْرُوفٌ, meaning 'well known:' and in a very large proportion of such cases, what was once 'well known' has long ceased to be so"; words "meant to be understood in post-classical senses". Guide attributes these to Lane about "such lexicons".
