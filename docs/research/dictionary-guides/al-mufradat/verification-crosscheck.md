## 1. Lede: "lexicon and commentary share the page, sometimes in a single sentence"

- Displayed Mufradāt sjd (entry_id 1465), first sentence: السُّجُودُ أصله: التّطامن والتّذلّل، وجعل ذلك عبارة عن التّذلّل لله وعبادته. A lexical origin ("lowering oneself, self-abasement") and its religious application ("made an expression for abasing oneself before God and worshipping Him") share one sentence.
- A second instance is the displayed kfr entry (entry_id 157) at Q 24:55: عني بالكافر السّاتر للحقّ، فلذلك جعله فاسقا. A gloss and an exegetical inference share one sentence.
- "Sometimes" is appropriately hedged. **Verdict: supported.**

## 2. "Al-Rāghib's *kāfir* of Q 24:55 'conceals the truth' (translation for this guide), as *kufr* does in Ibn Fāris"

- Mufradāt kfr (entry_id 157): وقوله: وَمَنْ كَفَرَ بَعْدَ ذلِكَ فَأُولئِكَ هُمُ الْفاسِقُونَ [النور/ 55] عني بالكافر السّاتر للحقّ. The verse fragment is indeed Q 24:55. He glosses it as "by the kāfir is meant the concealer of al-ḥaqq". "Al-Rāghib's kāfir" is fair because he himself names the kāfir.
- Maqāyīs kfr (entry_id 163): وَالْكُفْرُ: ضِدُّ الْإِيمَانِ، سُمِّيَ لِأَنَّهُ تَغْطِيَةُ الْحَقِّ, meaning kufr is named for being a covering-over of al-ḥaqq. The parallel holds: both works use al-ḥaqq as the thing covered.
- Nuance, no change made: al-Rāghib goes on to say ومعناه: من جحد حقّ الله فقد فسق, "whoever denies God's ḥaqq". Here ḥaqq can also be read as "God's due", which fits his thanks-based route. "The truth" is a standard and defensible rendering, and it is the one the site's own translation uses. The comparison with Ibn Fāris stands on the identical word. If anything, the ambiguity strengthens the sentence's "but".
- The label "translation for this guide" is now singular, which is correct because only one quotation is left in that paragraph. The preceding paragraph's plural label still covers its two quotations ("conventionally understood", "it has been said"). **Verdict: supported.**

## 3. "…but he reaches that sense through the Qur'an's pairing of *kufr* with thanks"

The displayed entry 157 runs in this order:
1. Physical covering.
2. كفر النعمة … سترها بترك أداء شكرها (Q 21:94).
3. Ingratitude set against shukr (Q 27:40, 2:152, 26:19, 14:7).
4. ولمّا كان الكفران يقتضي جحود النّعمة صار يستعمل في الجحود, with Q 2:41 glossed جاحد له وساتر.
5. The conventional religious kāfir.
6. kafara used for one who neglects the thanks owed to God (Q 30:44).
7. Q 24:55, "the concealer of al-ḥaqq".

So the route to "concealer" runs explicitly through ingratitude and denial.

Ibn Fāris (entry 163) derives kufr as faith's opposite directly from covering ("so named because it is a covering of the truth"). He adds ingratitude afterwards as a parallel (وَكَذَلِكَ كُفْرَانُ النِّعْمَةِ). The implied contrast is therefore accurate.

"Reaches that sense through" describes the entry's sequence. Al-Rāghib does not say in so many words that his 24:55 gloss derives from the shukr pairing, but his explicit statement that kufrān "came to be used for denial" and his gloss of Q 2:41 as "denier and concealer" support the reading. **Verdict: supported (interpretive, but grounded in the entry's own stated derivation).**
