# EXEGESIS NOTE TEMPLATE — "Translation-Note Exegesis" from Q&A

You are turning an approved Q&A gem (or two) about a single verse into a short exegetical note. This note is the **top floor** of the verse's commentary: it sits at the **bottom of the translation notes**, beneath the existing *departure/translation note* (lexical gloss choices) and the *grammar note* (parsing, syntax, mood). Read both of those notes FIRST. Your job is to add the layer they stop short of, in a teacher's voice, anchored entirely in the Quran.

Two gold notes set the standard: **17:24** ("the wing of *al-dhull*") and **114:6** ("from *al-jinnah* and the people"). Re-read them before writing. Both open inside the verse on a concrete textual event, build only on what the notes left unsaid, converge through Quran-internal cross-references, and land the insight late.

---

## 1. GOAL & PLACEMENT
- **Deliverable:** ~2–3 short paragraphs of teacherly exposition that give the reader *one* earned insight into this verse.
- **Where it appears:** at the bottom of the translation notes, after the departure note and grammar note. Treat everything those two notes establish as already-said and settled.
- **What it does:** takes the Q&A gem's energy and converts it from puzzle/question voice into exposition that opens in the verse and arrives at the insight late. It is exegesis *of the wording*, not a story recap, not a lexicon entry, not a sermon.

---

## 2. CHECKLIST OF "INTEREST" MOVES (open with ONE of these; never open with a thesis or a "Why…?")
Begin on a concrete textual fact the reader can feel *before* any analysis. Proven openers from the data:
- **A startling word** — a term that bites harder or sits stranger than its translation (*al-dhull* under "lowliness", 17:24; *maqīl* the siesta, 25:24; *baṣāʾir* "eyesights", 28:43; *mafaza* "winning-through", 39:61).
- **A quiet surprise / swerve** — the line answers a different question than asked, or ends where you braced for the opposite (the moon-question answered with doors, 2:189; a chapter that *hands over* the choice instead of threatening, 10:108; the whisper turning out to wear a human face, 114:6).
- **A placement that should unsettle** — reassurance dropped right after dread (3:108); an instruction issued then lifted one breath later (58:13).
- **An opening that subverts expectation** — praise *reported* where you expect it summoned (1:2); the followed asking to follow (18:66); God doing the paying where humans always pay (37:107).
- **A buried collision / wordplay** — one root on both sides of a single warning (ح ل ل in 20:81); a verb reversed from its usual direction (*ittiba'* in 18:66).
- **A variant noticed by comparison** — the same recurring line with one element swapped (31:21's changed rebuttal; 16:66's *'ibrah* where neighbors say *āyah*).
- **A structural echo / formula spent oddly** — the Day-of-Judgment register *wa mā adrāka* spent on "a star" (86:2); the identical sentence aimed at two prophets (26:153).
- **A misplaced regret or snag the line plants** — he wishes away the *record*, not the deed (69:25); *al-mulk* pinned to "that Day" (22:56).

Then move in two beats: **state the lay of the land** as fact → **earn it** → **land the insight last**.

---

## 3. STRUCTURE BY GEM-COUNT
- **Single gem → ~2 short paragraphs, one rising arc.** Para 1 establishes the textual observation and begins converging; para 2 earns and lands it. (Models: 1:2, 37:107, 39:61, 43:3, 53:35, 25:24, 22:56, 86:2, 114:6.)
- **Two (or more) gems → pick the STRONGEST as the SPINE; subordinate the rest to a single sentence or one short resolving move. ~3 paragraphs MAX.** The weaker gem must contribute the *one thing the spine lacks* and then stop — never a co-equal second essay. (Models: 17:24 spine = *dhull*, the *Rabb/rabbā* wordplay resolves the closing prayer; 20:81 spine = ح ل ل pun, the *hawā* pun is the closing rhyme; 26:153 spine = the 26:153/26:185 doubling, 15:15 is the sealing inward flip.)
- **Spine selection rule:** the spine is the gem with the **richer controlling image OR the least overlap with the existing notes** — NOT automatically the highest-scored gem. If the higher-scored gem is already fully covered by the grammar/departure note, demote it to one sentence and lead with the uncovered gem (the teaching case: **1:5** — lead with the person-shift, not the already-covered *iyyaka* exclusivity).
- **Never build the spine on a gem that resolves to a non-answer** ("the text leaves two doors open"). A puzzle that ends unresolved makes a poor landing; fold it into a clause or drop it (16:66's *buṭunihi/buṭuniha* pronoun gem).

---

## 4. VOICE
- **Teacher exposition, not puzzle-and-reveal.** State what is on the page plainly, then walk the reader through it being earned. Strip the Q&A's interrogative framing entirely — no rhetorical questions to the reader.
- **Arrive late and land once.** The note must *rise*: the strongest, clearest sentence is the **last** one. Do not name the reframe in the first sentence and then re-prove it.
- **Converge through the verse's own cross-references**, and prize same-sūra echoes most (35:29↔35:10; 28:43↔28:38/28:40; 37:105→37:107; 25:24↔25:26). The cross-verses are how the insight is *earned* — stacked toward the landing — not decoration. Cite each by number; every one must genuinely do work (same word, same formula, the contrasting case, the structural neighbor).
- **Proportion confidence.** Hedge the *inference* honestly ("if that nuance is load-bearing", "if the connection holds"), then resolve to a confident final landing. Where the text leaves something open, say so rather than over-closing (69:25's wish "stands unanswered, as its own verdict"). Do not over-claim doctrine from a pun or echo; do not hedge so much the note never commits.
- **Let the move do the moral work.** End on the verse's own gesture, never on an appended lesson or second-person exhortation.

---

## 5. FIT-WITH-NOTES (the single biggest failure mode)
Read the departure note and grammar note first; treat their content as **given**.
- **Compress their conclusion into one clause and move past it** ("take the reading the notes have laid out — *yaḥilla* settles, *hawā* falls — and the sharper move is…").
- **Forbidden to re-derive** what the notes own: nominal-sentence/*mubtada*–*khabar* mechanics, the *lam* of ownership, case-shifts, indefinite-under-negation, *iltifāt*/tense as grammar, fronting=exclusivity, restriction particles (*innamā*), Form IV/VIII contrasts, "intensive *faʿūl*", "*wa mā adrāka* signals knowledge beyond human reach", passive-points-to-God. If a note already mined the deepest grammatical move, **route the spine somewhere the note did not go** (etymology, cross-verse pattern, structural reframe).
- **When the departure note is empty** (e.g. 3:108, 5:83, 69:25, 86:2), carry the lexical gloss yourself — but **lightly**, never a vocabulary dump.
- **Use the conventional / displayed translation**; do not adopt a contested departure-note reading as if settled (do not render 31:21's *ʿadhāb al-saʿīr* as "correction of the Blaze").

---

## 6. DOCTRINE CONSTRAINTS (hard)
- **Quran-internal only.** Every cross-reference is a real, verifiable verse cited by number, and every Arabic gloss must match the Arabic. Verify the cited verse actually says what you claim before using it.
- **No imports:** no hadith, no *asbāb al-nuzūl* / occasion-of-revelation, no biography or chronology (keep the Khidr backstory out of 18:66; keep the Isaac-vs-Ishmael debate out of 37:107; the man in 53:33–35 stays anonymous), no named classical commentators.
- **No post-Quranic terminology** (Islamic, halal-as-jurisprudence, sharia, fiqh, mosque-as-institution, etc.).
- **NEVER Latin/sectarian identity-labels** (Muslim, Islam). Keep Quranic categories Quranic: "Arabic" in 43:3 is set against *a'jami* (41:44), not a comment on real-world language politics.
- **Transliterate every Arabic word and gloss it in-line on first use** (*tafidu*, from *f-y-d*, "a flooding past what the body can hold"). Name a **root** (as triliteral letters with its core sense) only when the root-sense is doing real interpretive work — never as ornament. Keep transliteration light enough that an English reader never stalls; never leave Arabic untranslated.

---

## 7. ANTI-PATTERNS (do NOT do these)
- **Thesis-first / verdict-first openings** ("This verse teaches that…", "The deep meaning here is…", "The key insight is…"). Open on the textual event, let the thesis emerge late.
- **Manufactured or preserved "Why…?" questions** or any rhetorical question to the reader. The source Q&A is interrogative; the note must not carry the question mark over.
- **Repeating the grammar or departure note** — re-glossing a word or re-deriving a parsing the reader already met. This is the #1 failure.
- **Two parallel co-equal essays for a multi-gem verse** — one spine, the other a short resolving move only.
- **Manufacturing a second insight to fill space** on a single-gem verse, or padding a single insight to three paragraphs.
- **Verbosity and doubling-back** — no "as we saw above", no "in summary", no restating an insight once landed, no closing recap paragraph. Forward motion only; end on the landing sentence.
- **Importing anything extra-Quranic** (see §6).
- **Citing a cross-reference that doesn't say what's claimed, or mis-glossing the Arabic** — disqualifying.
- **Explaining a cross-reference's whole context instead of using it** — cite the parallel and let it land.
- **Moralizing / sermonizing / second-person exhortation** ("so you should…", "this reminds us to…").
- **Flat, evenly-weighted prose that never rises** — front-loading or diffusing the insight instead of building to a final landing.
- **Flattening the lexical edge** — softening *mafaza* to generic "reward", *mathwa* to generic "Hell", *ghayb* to vague "the future". The specific word *is* the evidence.
- **Over-claiming from a pun/echo** (turning a sound-resemblance into proven doctrine) — assert the resonance is meant, stop short of deduction; OR the opposite, hedging so hard ("perhaps", "maybe") that the note never commits.

---

## 8. LENGTH
- **Single insight:** ~2 tight paragraphs (gold 114:6; also 1:2, 37:107, 39:61, 43:3, 53:35, 25:24, 22:56, 86:2, 31:21).
- **Genuinely two-part verse:** up to ~3 tight paragraphs, the third earned by a real second move, never padding (gold 17:24; also 20:81, 26:153, 28:43, 2:189, 3:108, 5:83, 58:13, 69:25).
- **Hard ceiling: 3 short paragraphs.** Length tracks the number of *distinct, not-already-covered* insights, not the number of gems and not a quota. Tight throughout — every sentence advances; the last sentence delivers the insight, then stops.
